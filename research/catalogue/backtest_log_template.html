How to read the numbers

Out of sample (OOS)
Data the strategy was not built or tuned on. Only out-of-sample results count.
Walk-forward
Pick settings on an early stretch of data, then test them on the later stretch without changing anything.
t-stat (t)
How far a result is from zero in units of its own noise. Around 2 or more is hard to get by luck in a single test; much less so when many tests were run.
DSR
Deflated Sharpe ratio: the probability the edge is real after correcting for how many variants were tried. I require 0.95 or more.
Drop-top-1
Remove the single best trade. If the result turns negative, one lucky trade was carrying it.
Random-entry control
The same stops, targets and hold time with a random or reversed entry. If that does as well, the signal adds nothing.
R
Profit or loss measured in units of the amount risked per trade. +0.1R per trade means 10% of the risk, on average.
bp
Basis points: hundredths of a percent. 10bp = 0.10%.
Forward test
Trading the frozen rules on new data as it arrives. The final check, and the one that sank my best backtest.
Prop account limits
Several studies target prop-firm evaluations, which cap daily loss and total drawdown. A strategy can be profitable and still fail those caps.
Research log, not financial advice. Results are backtests on historical data; they describe what happened, not what will. — @Purrtfolio

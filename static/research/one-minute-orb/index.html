The one-minute ORB scalp at 3:1

Scalp the first 1- and 2-minute range off the open, fixed 3:1, and win 34–36% of the time. A Reddit scalping post, claiming five years of results

On real tick data it won 26.1% of trades. That is almost exactly what a coin flip gives a 3:1 trade, and after costs it made nothing.

The trade

How it works

  1. Mark the first candleThe high and low of 09:30–09:31 ET. A second trade uses the first two minutes, 09:30–09:32.
  2. Wait for a break before 09:35Price must trade outside the range between 09:31 and 09:35 ET. No break, no trade.
  3. Enter in the direction of the breakEach range is traded once. Skip the 2-minute trade while the 1-minute trade is still open.
  4. Stop at the other side of the rangeThat distance is your risk, 1R.
  5. Target 3RIf neither level is hit, close at 16:00.
The thesis

Why it should work

The open is when orders land

Overnight news and queued orders hit the market in the first minutes. A clean break of the first candle can show which side is in control.

Big orders take time

Institutions working large orders keep pushing in the same direction for a while, so an early move can carry on.

3:1 means you can be wrong a lot

With winners three times the size of losers, you break even at a 25% win rate. Anything above that is profit, before costs.

expectancy = 3 × win% − (1 − win%) At the claimed 34–36%, that is +0.36R to +0.44R per trade. A real edge that size would be excellent.
Before looking at results

What does a coin flip win at each R:R?

If price moves randomly, a bracket with the stop 1 unit away and the target k units away is hit on the target side 1 / (1 + k) of the time. That is the bar any setup has to beat.

Trading with the break beat trading against it by +0.31R per trade (t = 2.50). So opening momentum is real.

But the gross edge is about 0.12R, and the round-trip cost is 0.09R (0.57 points of spread on a typical 6-point stop). Even with zero costs it isn't statistically significant (t = 1.57), so a cheaper broker or futures would not rescue it.

On a prop account

The losing streaks break the drawdown limit

A 26% win rate means long losing runs: 15 in a row at worst, and a 32.7R drawdown. Risking 0.5% per trade, that is a 16.4% drawdown, well past a typical 10% limit. At 0.25% per trade it stays inside the limit, but earns about 0.5% a year against a 10% target.

The lesson

Before believing any win rate on a fixed R:R trade, compare it with 1 / (1 + R:R). A 26% win rate at 3:1 is what randomness produces.

How it was tested

FTMO S&P 500 CFD tick data, 519 sessions (about 190 quotes a minute at the open), so the order in which the stop and target were hit is observed, not assumed, and every trade pays the real spread. The decision rule was written down before any results were looked at. Checked against the same trade in the opposite direction and with a random direction.
